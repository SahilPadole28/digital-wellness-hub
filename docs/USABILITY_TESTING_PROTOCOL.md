# Usability Testing Protocol — Digital Wellness Resource Hub
**Project 3 · Digital Wellness Course**

## Overview
Conduct usability testing with **3–5 student participants** to identify problems and revise the site before submission. This protocol covers consent, tasks, observation, and post-test questions.

**Duration per session:** approximately 30–40 minutes
**Format:** Think-aloud protocol (participant narrates what they are doing and thinking)
**Mode:** In-person preferred; can be done remotely via screen share

---

## Consent Wording

Read to participant before beginning:

> "We're testing a website, not testing you — there are no right or wrong answers. We want to understand how easy the site is to use, so your honest reactions help us improve it. The session will take about 30 minutes. We'll record your screen and voice to review later, but only the research team will see the recording. It will be deleted after we complete the report. We'll refer to you only as [P1/P2/etc.] in any written notes. You can stop at any time. Do you have any questions before we begin?"

**Consent statement (participant signs or verbally confirms):**

> "I agree to participate in this usability session. I understand that my participation is voluntary and that my identity will not be shared in any public document."

Participant code (do not record name): P1 / P2 / P3 / P4 / P5

---

## Neutral Script

Before each task, read aloud (do not lead or hint):

> "Please try to think aloud — tell me what you are looking at, what you are thinking, and what you are trying to do. I won't be able to help you during the task, but I am here if you have any questions after."

---

## Task 1: Find a suitable resource

**Goal:** Evaluate whether users can identify a relevant tool for their own concern.

**Prompt (read exactly):**

> "Think of something you've found difficult about your own digital habits — maybe you spend too much time on your phone, have trouble concentrating, or are concerned about your privacy online. Using this website, find a tool that you think could help with that specific problem. Tell me what you are doing as you go."

## Task 2: Follow one setup guide

**Goal:** Evaluate whether guide steps are clear and actionable.

**Prompt (read exactly):**

> "Now please find the setup guide for [name a guide relevant to what they found in Task 1, or choose one]. Read through the guide and tell me what it's asking you to do. If you have a device with you, try following the first three steps."

## Post-test Questions

After both tasks, ask the participant (record answers verbatim or paraphrased):

1. **Overall impression:** "What was your first impression of the website?"
2. **Most helpful:** "Which part of the site did you find most useful?"
3. **Confusion:** "Was there anything that confused you or that you weren't sure how to use?"
4. **Trust:** "Did you feel the tool information was trustworthy? Why or why not?"
5. **Missing:** "Is there anything you would have liked the site to include that wasn't there?"
6. **Return:** "Would you use this site to find a digital wellness tool? Why or why not?"

Severity guide:
- **High:** Participant cannot complete core task; causes significant confusion or failure
- **Medium:** Causes delay or confusion but task completed eventually
- **Low:** Minor friction; participant copes but comments negatively
